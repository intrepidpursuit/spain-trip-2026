If you go to Lloret

Around Hotel Santa Marta

The hotel sits in pine woods above Santa Cristina beach at the far southern end of Lloret, roughly 3 km from the town centre and well clear of it. Almost everything worth seeing nearby is reachable on foot along the coast path; the rest is a short drive. Times below are approximate.

Jardins de Santa Clotilde
~15 min walk · €6.10 adult, €3.10 reduced · open daily 10:00–20:00 in September · allow 1.5–2 hrs

The best thing near the hotel by a wide margin, and it's essentially next door — laid out along the clifftop directly between Fenals and Sa Boadella. Commissioned by the Marquis of Roviralta and designed in 1919 by Nicolau Rubió i Tudurí, in the manner of an Italian Renaissance garden: cypress avenues, statuary, ponds, and long staircases that drop through the pines toward the water, framing the sea at the bottom. Quiet, shaded, and genuinely beautiful rather than merely pleasant.

Platja de Santa Cristina
At the hotel · direct access from the grounds

The hotel's own beach — broad, sheltered, backed by umbrella pines, with a hermitage on the headland above. Reached straight down from the gardens, which is why it stays calm: there's no through road, and the only casual arrivals come by boat or on foot.

Cala Sa Boadella
~10 min walk over the headland

The next cove along, down a wooded path. Effectively the only undeveloped beach left in Lloret — no road, no buildings, pines running to the sand, and a single small chiringuito. Regularly named among the finest coves on the Costa Brava. Quiet on a weekday, and quieter still at the far end.

Camí de Ronda
Passes the hotel · 8.5 km south to Blanes, 11 km north to Tossa

The old coastguard path, cut into the cliffs and running the length of this coast. The stretch outside the hotel links the Santa Clotilde gardens to Santa Cristina by way of Boadella, so the three sit on one walk. You can pick up as much or as little of it as you want — it's the most rewarding hour available without getting in a car.

Castell de Sant Joan
~10 min drive · modest entry fee · allow 1–1.5 hrs

An 11th-century watchtower on the hill between Lloret and Fenals beaches, knocked about by British bombardment in 1805 and restored since. Panoramic views up and down the coast. Dan will want this one.

Castell d'en Plaja
~10 min drive, or along the seafront on foot

The castle you've seen in every photo of Lloret — neo-Gothic, circular towers and pointed windows, planted on the rocks at Sa Caleta. It's actually 20th-century and privately built, which nobody mentions. Best at the end of the day.

Lloret old town
~10 min drive · half a morning

Worth an hour despite the town's reputation. Sant Romà church, begun in 1509 and reworked in 1914, wears bright trencadís mosaic domes. Can Font is an 1887 indiano house museum — built with money made in the Americas — and the only one of its kind preserved in Catalonia. The Modernist Cemetery on the hill is a listed collection of funerary architecture and much stranger and better than it sounds.

Tossa de Mar
25 min drive north

The cliff road there is half the reason to go. A walled medieval old town standing directly on the sea, with towers you can climb and a beach inside the walls. The best half-day from Lloret.

Blanes
20 min drive south

Chiefly for the Marimurtra botanical garden, laid out on the cliffs above the sea and among the most important Mediterranean collections in Europe. Also Cala Sant Francesc — the pine-backed cove with the café that's on Jessi's list — and another Castell de Sant Joan above the town.
