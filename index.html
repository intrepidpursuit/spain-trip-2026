Sat 20 – Mon 21 September

It starts in the air

An overnight nonstop out of Atlanta, a lie-flat seat, and Barcelona at ten to one the next afternoon. That first day is really half a day: check in at the Grand Hotel Central on Via Laietana, then walk the Gothic Quarter once the heat has gone out of it.

Nobody else has landed yet. This is the only part of the trip that belongs entirely to the two of you.

Tue 22 – Wed 23 September

Everyone arrives

Devon, Tasia and Scott come in on the same routing a day behind, and the trip becomes a group thing at quarter past three at the Mirador de Joan Sales. Park Güell, then dinner in Gràcia.

The next morning is the Sagrada Família at ten fifteen — ninety minutes minimum, and the interior is the reason, not the facade. Sant Pau in the afternoon, twelve minutes up Avinguda Gaudí and far quieter.

Thursday 24 September

North, with a castle in the middle

The tightest morning of the trip. Check out at quarter to ten, collect the car at Sants at half past — it's an electric Citroën, parked underground on level minus one — and be on the coast road by ten past eleven.

Lunch at Canet de Mar, then Castell de Santa Florentina at half one: an eleventh-century manor that Domènech i Montaner turned into something closer to a fantasy, and that Game of Thrones used as Horn Hill. Girona by a quarter to four.

The full day →
Friday 25 September

Girona, at its own pace

Free until the evening. The old town is small enough to do properly in an afternoon — coloured houses over the Onyar, one of the best-preserved Jewish quarters in Europe, and city walls you can walk end to end for nothing.

The meet and greet is downstairs at the hotel, which means the one evening with a fixed start needs no transport at all.

Sat 26 – Sun 27 September

The reason for all of it

Jessica and Daniel, four o'clock, at a restored farmhouse in the countryside north of Girona. Hair and makeup from six in the morning, portraits at two, and the whole day happens in one place.

Sunday is deliberately empty. Brunch from ten for whoever surfaces, then the rooftop pool and the video recap.

The full day →
Monday 28 September

Vines, then the sea

Check out and drive north — the winery is above Girona, not below it, which is what makes this the longest driving day of the trip. Three hours at Clos de Basella: a walk through the vines, the cellar, and a picnic that is the point rather than an add-on.

At three it ends and the group splits. The Raymonds turn back to Barcelona; you carry on south to a single night at Hotel Santa Marta, in pine woods above its own beach and three kilometres clear of Lloret proper.

The full day →
Tuesday 29 September

One good morning on the coast

The gardens at Santa Clotilde open at ten and are essentially next door — cypress avenues and long staircases dropping through the pines to frame the sea. Santa Cristina is below the hotel and Sa Boadella is the next cove over the headland, so all three sit on one walk.

Leave at quarter past three, hand the car back at Sants at five, and rejoin the Raymonds in Barcelona for the evening.

The full day →
Wednesday 30 September

Figueres

The Dalí Theatre-Museum, built inside the bombed-out shell of the town theatre and designed room by room as a single work. He's buried in the basement. An hour on the AVE and no car required — which matters, because the museum shuts on Mondays through September and that closure is what reshaped this whole week.

The full day →
Thu 1 – Fri 2 October

The last days together, then the last day alone

Thursday is the Raymonds' final full day, so it stays in the city: El Born, Santa Maria del Mar, the Picasso museum three minutes away, and a farewell dinner.

They fly at eleven on Friday. After the goodbyes, Montserrat — a monastery wedged into a mountain of pink conglomerate, and the one piece of real landscape within reach of Barcelona. Doing it now means coming home to a packed suitcase and an easy morning.

Home
