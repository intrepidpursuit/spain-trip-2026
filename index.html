Park Güell

Gaudí's failed luxury housing estate, now the most joyful thing in the city — a serpentine mosaic bench, a hall of tilted columns, and a terrace looking clean over Barcelona to the sea. The ticketed zone is small; the free park around it is large and worth the extra hour.

Basílica de la Sagrada Família

Under construction since 1882. The exterior is the famous part; the interior is the reason to go — a stone forest where the columns branch overhead and the glass throws red light west and blue east. Ninety minutes minimum. Your ticket is basilica only, no towers.

Maps ↗ GaudíUnmissable
Sant Pau Recinte Modernista

A working hospital until 2009, built as twenty-seven mosaic-and-brick pavilions in a garden — Domènech i Montaner's answer to Gaudí, and to many people the better building. Far quieter than the Sagrada. Jessi may hold tickets for five; ask before buying.

Castell de Santa Florentina

An 11th-century fortified manor at Canet de Mar, reworked by Domènech i Montaner into something between a castle and a Modernista fantasy. Still privately owned and lived in. Doubled as Horn Hill in Game of Thrones. Tickets weren't on sale as of August — worth checking.

Girona Old Town

Coloured houses stacked over the Onyar, one of Europe's best-preserved Jewish quarters, 12th-century Arab baths, and an iron footbridge Gustave Eiffel built before the tower. The medieval walls are free to walk end to end.

Girona Cathedral

Ninety-one steps up from the square, and the widest Gothic nave in the world at 23 metres — built without side aisles because the architects argued and the ambitious side won. The staircase is the one from the Great Sept.

Jardins de Santa Clotilde

The best thing near the hotel by a wide margin, and essentially next door — laid out along the clifftop in 1919 by Nicolau Rubió i Tudurí in the manner of an Italian Renaissance garden. Cypress avenues, statuary, ponds, and long staircases dropping through the pines to frame the sea. €6.10, open to 8pm in September.

Maps ↗ Gardens15 min walk
Platja de Santa Cristina & Cala Sa Boadella

Santa Cristina is the hotel's own beach — broad, sheltered, backed by umbrella pines, with a hermitage on the headland. Sa Boadella is the next cove over the headland, ten minutes down a wooded path: no road, no buildings, pines to the sand, one small chiringuito. Regularly named among the finest coves on the Costa Brava.

Teatre-Museu Dalí

Dalí built it inside the bombed-out shell of the town theatre and designed every room as a single work — the Mae West face you assemble by standing in the right spot, the Rainy Taxi in the courtyard, giant eggs along the roofline. He's buried in the basement. Not a museum of his paintings so much as the largest surrealist object he ever made.

Santa Maria del Mar

Built in fifty-five years by the parish itself, which is why it's all one style — bare, tall, astonishingly plain Catalan Gothic with almost nothing in it. The anarchists burned the baroque fittings in 1936 and the building was better for it. Free most of the day.

Museu Picasso

Five medieval palaces knocked together, holding the early work almost nobody associates with him — academic paintings made at fifteen, the Blue Period, and the full Las Meninas series. Book ahead; it sells out.

Montserrat

A Benedictine monastery wedged into a mountain of pink conglomerate that erodes into hundreds of rounded fingers, visible from half of Catalonia. The Black Madonna is inside; the boys' choir sings most days around 1pm. The real draw is above the abbey — funiculars and ridge paths with a thousand-metre drop to the plain.

Sitges

Thirty-five minutes on the R2 from Passeig de Gràcia, no car and no planning. Whitewashed seaside town, seventeen beaches, a Modernista museum quarter. The lowest-effort day here.

Cadaqués & Portlligat

The fuller Dalí. Portlligat is the house he lived in — built from fishermen's huts over forty years, egg on the roof. Two hours past Figueres, so only works as an overnight.

Penedès cava country

Forty-five minutes to Sant Sadurní. Cellars cut under the vineyards — Codorníu's are a Modernista landmark — plus small growers doing serious work. You'll have had two wine days by then.

Full day · 1h by train
Tarragona

The Roman capital of the peninsula — an amphitheatre built directly above the beach, a surviving circus under the medieval town, an aqueduct outside the city. Best Roman ruins in Catalonia, almost no queues.

Maps ↗
Slots into Sep 25 · near Girona
Besalú & Banyoles

Twenty minutes from Girona. Besalú is an intact medieval town entered across a fortified bridge, with one of the only surviving Jewish ritual baths in Europe. Banyoles has Catalonia's largest lake and a flat hour-long walk around it, very good at sunset.

Maps ↗
Any Barcelona morning · book ahead
Casa Batlló & La Pedrera

Two Gaudí houses ten minutes apart on Passeig de Gràcia. Batlló is theatrical — bone balconies, scaled dragon roof, a light well that darkens as it rises. La Pedrera is austere, and its rooftop of armoured chimneys is the better view. Both sell out days ahead.

Maps ↗
An evening · free
Montjuïc & the Magic Fountain

The hill above the port: gardens, the 1929 exposition palace holding the world's best Romanesque fresco collection, a castle at the top by cable car, and the fountain light show below, free on selected evenings.

Maps ↗
From Lloret · 25 min
Tossa de Mar

The cliff road there is half the reason to go. A walled medieval old town standing directly on the sea, towers you can climb, and a beach inside the walls. The best short outing from Lloret if the one morning stretches.
