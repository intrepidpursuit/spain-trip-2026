Park Güell

Gaudí's failed luxury housing estate, now the most joyful thing in the city — a serpentine mosaic bench, a hall of tilted columns, and a terrace looking clean over Barcelona to the sea. The ticketed monumental zone is small; the free park around it is large and worth the extra hour.

→ Sagrada Família → Sant Pau · 12 min · on foot, straight up Avinguda Gaudí

Basílica de la Sagrada Família

Gaudí's unfinished cathedral, under construction since 1882. The exterior is the famous part; the interior is the reason to go — a stone forest where the columns branch overhead and the stained glass throws red light west and blue light east. Give it 90 minutes minimum.

GaudíArchitectureUnmissable
Sant Pau Recinte Modernista

A working hospital until 2009, built as twenty-seven separate mosaic-and-brick pavilions in a garden — Domènech i Montaner's answer to Gaudí, and to many people the better building. Far quieter than the Sagrada. Tickets are bought separately on the site.

Castell de Santa Florentina

Girona Old Town

Coloured houses stacked over the Onyar, one of Europe's best-preserved Jewish quarters, 12th-century Arab baths, and an iron footbridge built by Gustave Eiffel before the tower. You can walk the medieval walls end to end for free.

Girona Cathedral

Ninety-one steps up from the square, and the widest Gothic nave in the world at 23 metres — built without side aisles because the architects argued and the ambitious side won. The staircase is the one from the Great Sept.

Lloret · a day that goes nowhere
→ Nothing scheduled · everything on foot from the hotel
The one genuinely idle day of the trip, and the group's too — their itinerary says only "relax, explore, enjoy." Hotel Santa Marta sits in pine woods above its own beach at the quiet southern end of Lloret, well away from the town. The coves either side are a short walk through the trees.
Platja de Santa Cristina

The hotel's own beach — broad, sheltered, backed by umbrella pines, with a hermitage on the headland above it. Reached directly from the grounds. The calmest sand in Lloret by a distance.

BeachAt the hotel
Cala Sa Boadella

The next cove along, over the headland and down a wooded path — ten minutes on foot from the hotel. Effectively the only undeveloped beach left in Lloret: no buildings, no road, pines to the waterline, and a single small chiringuito. Widely rated among the finest coves on the Costa Brava. Quiet on a weekday, and quieter at the far end.

BeachSecluded10 min walk
Tossa de Mar — if you want an outing

Twenty-five minutes up a cliff road that is itself the attraction. A walled medieval old town sitting directly on the sea, with towers you can climb. The best half-day from Lloret if the beach starts to feel like enough.

Santa Maria del Mar

Built in fifty-five years by the parish itself, which is why it is all one style — bare, tall, astonishingly plain Catalan Gothic with almost nothing in it. The anarchists burned the baroque fittings in 1936 and the building was better for it. Free most of the day.

GothicFree
Museu Picasso

Five medieval palaces knocked together, holding the early work almost no one associates with him — academic paintings made at fifteen, the Blue Period, and the full Las Meninas series. Book ahead; it sells out.

Teatre-Museu Dalí

Dalí built it inside the bombed-out shell of the town theatre and designed every room as a single work — the Mae West face you assemble by standing in the right spot, the Rainy Taxi in the courtyard, giant eggs along the roofline. He is buried in the basement. Not a museum of his paintings so much as the largest surrealist object he ever made.

Montserrat

A Benedictine monastery wedged into a mountain of pink conglomerate rock that erodes into hundreds of rounded fingers — visible from half of Catalonia. The Black Madonna is inside; the boys' choir sings most days around 1pm. The real draw is above the abbey: funiculars and marked ridge paths with a thousand-metre drop to the plain.

Alternates

Everything here is within a two-hour radius of Barcelona and slots into an open day without disturbing anything booked. Ordered roughly by how easy they are to add.

Swap for Montserrat · Oct 2
Sitges

Thirty-five minutes on the R2 from Passeig de Gràcia, no car and no planning. A whitewashed seaside town with seventeen beaches, a Modernista museum quarter and an easy, unbothered atmosphere. The lowest-effort day on this list, and the best one if Oct 2 turns out to want a beach rather than a mountain.

Needs a car + a night
Cadaqués & Portlligat

The fuller version of the Dalí day. Portlligat is the house he actually lived in — built out of fishermen's huts over forty years, with the egg on the roof — and Cadaqués is a whitewashed village at the end of a mountain road. Two hours past Figueres, so it only works as an overnight, which is what makes it the thing you'd add if you decide to rent a car.

Half day · any open afternoon
Penedès cava country

Forty-five minutes by train to Sant Sadurní. Miles of cellars cut under the vineyards — Codorníu's are a Modernista landmark in their own right — plus small growers doing serious work. Worth knowing you'll already have had two wine days by then.

Full day · 1h by train
Tarragona

The Roman capital of the peninsula, with an amphitheatre built directly above the beach, a surviving circus under the medieval town, and an aqueduct outside the city. The best Roman ruins in Catalonia and almost no queues.

Slots into Sep 25 · near Girona
Besalú & Banyoles

Twenty minutes from Girona. Besalú is an intact medieval town entered across a fortified stone bridge, with one of the only surviving Jewish ritual baths in Europe. Banyoles has the largest lake in Catalonia and a flat hour-long walk around it that is very good at sunset. The obvious escape if Girona feels too small for a full day.

Any Barcelona morning · book ahead
Casa Batlló & La Pedrera

Two Gaudí houses ten minutes apart on Passeig de Gràcia. Batlló is the theatrical one — bone balconies, a scaled dragon roof, a light well that darkens as it rises. La Pedrera is the austere one, and its rooftop of armoured chimneys is the better view. Both sell out days ahead.

An evening · free
Montjuïc & the Magic Fountain

The hill above the port: gardens, the 1929 exposition palace holding the world's best Romanesque fresco collection, a castle at the top reached by cable car, and the fountain light-and-music show below, which is free and runs on selected evenings. A good use of a night when nothing else is on.

A few hours · in the city
The city beaches

Barceloneta is closest and busiest. Keep walking northeast past the port and it gets steadily calmer through Bogatell to Mar Bella in Poblenou — young, mixed, and a fifteen-minute metro ride from anywhere central. Useful for an afternoon that doesn't need a plan.
